Graham Platner Was Never the Answer

Ben Kelly · July 9, 2026 · 5 min read

As Graham Platner’s nightmare of a campaign comes to an ugly end, it is important to look back and see how we got here. Some left him after the Nazi tattoo, some after the Reddit posts, some after the cheating, but others stuck by him until the rape accusation. I believe even if none of those details ever became public that Platner would still have underperformed in Maine. His candidacy rested on a flawed premise — that Maine wants a firebrand, anti-establishment progressive.

Supporters would argue that Maine showed exactly that desire with Platner’s win in the primary, but a primary is different from a general. To his credit, Platner was able to run a very effective campaign to win the Democratic primary. Unfortunately, that is only the start of the battle.

Plenty of candidates have won primaries only to see their message not translate to the general election. Look at Christine O’Donnell, who in 2010 beat the popular sitting Congressman Mike Castle for the nomination for Senate in Delaware with the support of the Tea Party. Castle led his Democratic opponent by double digits in every poll, but he narrowly lost to O’Donnell in the primary. She then went on to lose what was at one point a certain gain for the GOP.

Platner did not even face a particularly strong primary field. Governor Janet Mills entered the race late and ran a half-hearted campaign and most of the other potential candidates decided to run for Governor instead of Senate. In the general, he would face a formidable opponent in Susan Collins. Collins was outraising him and had more cash on hand at the time he dropped out and was ahead of him in the polls. Crucially, she is also outperforming Platner with white working class voters, which was supposedly Platner’s base. Susan Collins is a soft spoken Senator who styles herself as a moderate¹ focused on bipartisanship and she was able to win over the non-college educated voters better than Platner.

Platner and his team would argue that Maine does not want some moderate dealmaker, they want a fighter. However, look at who has been winning elections in Maine. Susan Collins has won every six years since 1996. Their Governor is Janet Mills, who Platner’s campaign would call an establishment corporatist. The other Senator from Maine is Angus King, an Independent who caucuses with the Democrats. Senator King has done a good job separating himself from the Democratic brand in Maine, but he has done so by appealing to the center, not the left, and was ranked in 2023 as the fifth most bipartisan Senator. Congressman Jared Golden represents Maine’s second district and is the chair of the Blue Dog Coalition, a group of moderate to conservative Democrats. Where is this supposed progressive movement in Maine? It has not shown itself anywhere outside of Democratic primaries.

This is also true for other purple-to-red states. In North Carolina, moderate former Governor Roy Cooper is leading his Republican opponent in almost every poll. Is Cooper some firebrand progressive who talks about fighting the oligarchy? No, he is a centrist who is focused on cost of living and healthcare. He gets along with the party establishment and does not spend his time ranting about Chuck Schumer or the billionaires, yet he will most likely win a Senate seat in a state that voted for Donald Trump just two years ago.

The argument that Platner needed to be a different kind of Democrat is also flawed because Kamala Harris won Maine. The Democratic establishment that Platner was decrying had just won the state two years ago. If Platner just appealed to Harris voters, he would have won the general with no issue.

All of this would have been true even without his scandals. But as the scandals kept coming out and became worse and worse, it showed that there was a major flaw in candidate recruitment. It is clear that he is someone who appeals to young, progressive, college educated voters. He is an white collar worker’s idea of what a blue collar man looks like. He wears jeans, has facial hair and tattoos, and swears a lot. This is the same line of thinking that got young progressives to support John Fetterman and ignore his obvious red flags². In reality, politicians of all types can find different ways to appeal to working class voters. Susan Collins, a 73 year old woman who has spent the last thirty years in DC, was outperforming the gruff veteran outsider among working class voters.

Platner’s campaign ran a perfunctory vetting process that missed many of his controversies and seemed to not care about major ones such as his Reddit posts. For context, his Reddit posts had many disturbing elements, perhaps the most horrifying one was from 2013 and blamed women for getting too drunk and being raped. At the time he posted that, Platner was a 29 year old man.

Yes, everyone has said stupid or terrible things in their life (though I would argue that Platner’s statements are more egregious than usual). But Platner did not stop there. Even after assuring people there were no more skeletons in his closet, we saw his Nazi tattoo. I do not think Graham Platner is a secret Nazi, but I do think the tattoo shows he is a man with terrible judgement who should not be trusted to make important decisions for our country. Then it came out that he was cheating on his wife at the start of the campaign, then the allegations of abuse from an ex girlfriend came out, and finally the accusation of rape from a different ex girlfriend was brought to light. It is shocking, but not at all surprising.

Even his video announcing his withdrawal showed him to be an unsavory person. He made no apologies and said that he was only leaving because the establishment was forcing him to do so. If the establishment has a policy of forcing rapists out, I support the establishment on that one.

Outsiders can make great candidates and politicians. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez was a bartender just before going to Congress, and I think her experience has made her a talented communicator and she has shown an ability to adapt and grow in office. One of Barack Obama’s main appeals in his 2008 race was that he was not a creature of Washington and had only been in the Senate for four years. Nobody would argue that “the establishment” is popular or that candidates should tie themselves to it, but the opposite of the establishment is not some creep who posts about rape on Reddit. Platner was not the guy for this campaign and that was clear long ago.

The Main Democratic Party has a real opportunity to switch things around here. Prediction markets are hardly a scientific measure, but it is telling that on both Kalshi and Polymarket the chance of a Democratic victory in Maine increased after Platner withdrew.

I do not pretend to know who would be the right fit for Maine or what faction of the party should decide the new nominee. All I know is that it should be someone who appeals to Mainers of all political stripes, not just overly online firebrands.

¹Emphasis on the words “styles herself”. Collins’ record does not match her moderate claims.

²Full transparency: I am one of those young college educated progressives who supported Fetterman in 2022.
